How to read this. The 12NC’s first two digits are the main product group; digits 3–4 the centre of issue (often a country); digits 5–6 can add a subgroup. The two assignment columns quote the 1964 and 1968 standards so they can be read side by side — much of the 1968 wording refines rather than replaces the 1964 meaning. Assignments are quoted for research and reference; primary documents courtesy of the Philips Company Archives. Author notes are the author’s own observations from parts seen in use, not archival statements. A working reconstruction — corrections welcome.
The alphanumeric coding system Philips used for consumer electronics before the 12NC — letters encode product type, production origin, and power supply; a digit indicates price class; a two-digit group usually gives the year.

How to read this. A typical pre-12NC product code looks like TX3 or BX5/25. The first letter gives the product type; the second the production origin; the third the power supply; the digit a price class. An optional slash-suffix can denote development centre, market, or a technical modification — its meaning varied by product line.
